Lesson 5 · 16 min

Where the money is: software became a commodity, the method didn't

Today an agent can reverse-engineer almost any software, and an automation takes minutes. Selling that on its own got cheap. What repeats and holds value is the method: lead generation, management and administration connected, documented and packaged to apply to any business.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The three blocks of a business: lead generation, management, administration
  2. 2Find the real bottleneck
  3. 3The parts of a lead generation method
  4. 4Package and document: do it once, apply it always

Goal: document the parts of your lead generation method and write the step-by-step of one of them so you can repeat it in another business.

01 · A business in three blocks

Tap each block. The bottleneck is almost always in the first one.

With management and administrative solved, you are free to focus on lead generation
Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Don't build a product just to build one: lean on ready-made tools (CRM, accounting) and spend your effort on the method.

02 · From software to method

Four ideas.

1. Commodity

Standalone automation is built in minutes with an agent. Software can be cloned by reverse engineering.

If your product is only that, the price will drop.

2. Leverage

For CRM, accounting and scheduling, there are ready-made tools. Connect them and customize.

Building your own CRM takes months that should go to lead generation.

3. Method

A method is the sequence of pieces you apply the same way in any business: a real estate agency, a clinic, an office.

A well-built lead generation method runs the same in different businesses. The angle changes; the structure stays.

4. Package

Each piece has a written step-by-step (runbook) and, when possible, a ready-made skill or template for the agent.

At the end, a verifier agent checks that all the pieces are active.

03 · Templates to copy

The list is a starting point. Your method will have your own pieces.

METODO.md · lead generation pieces
# Lead generation method · <name of your method>

AD AND PAGE
[ ] 1. angles defined (at least 3), each with the customer's pain point
[ ] 2. static ads per angle, in each format
[ ] 3. landing page with text that changes according to the angle (utm_content)
[ ] 4. UTMs and URLs generated by rule, never by hand

CRM
[ ] 5. landing page connected to the CRM, always with the same custom fields
[ ] 6. opportunities that move through stages on their own based on events
[ ] 7. conversion events sent back to the ad platform
[ ] 8. service assistant that opens the conversation with the same angle

SALES
[ ] 9. call scheduling straight into the salesperson's calendar
[ ] 10. call evaluator (score and reason for each call)
[ ] 11. sales playbook that updates itself with the best calls

CONTROL
[ ] 12. agent that measures which angles work
[ ] 13. verifier agent: are all the pieces active?

Each piece has its own runbook in runbooks/<piece>.md
runbooks/[piece].md
# Runbook · <name of the piece>

WHAT IT'S FOR: <one line>
INPUT: <what must exist beforehand>
OUTPUT: <what exists afterward, and where>

STEP BY STEP
1. <step>
2. <step>
3. <step>

HOW TO CHECK: <what to look at to know it worked>
ERRORS ALREADY SEEN: <date · what went wrong · how to avoid it>

04 · Practice

Think about the business you serve or want to serve.

1 · Where the bottleneck is

10 min

Write down the business's three blocks (lead generation, management, administrative) and give each a score from 0 to 10 for how organized it is.

Done when: you have the three scores and can say which block is the bottleneck.

2 · METODO.md

40 min

Copy the list of pieces, remove what doesn't fit, add what's yours, and mark what already exists.

Done when: METODO.md has at least 8 pieces, each one marked as exists or missing.

3 · A runbook

30 min

Pick a piece you already do and write its runbook using the template.

Done when: the runbook has an input, an output, at least 3 steps, and a "how to check".
